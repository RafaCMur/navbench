A*

Lays a grid of 5 cm squares over the house and crosses out the squares the robot does not fit in. Then it searches outward from the start, always trying the most promising square first. It finds the shortest route on that grid, and the same route every time.

RRT*

Picks random points in the free space and grows two trees of branches toward them, one from the start and one from the goal. When it finds a shorter link between branches, it swaps it in. It stops when the two trees meet, so the route depends on where the random points land.

What I found

On average A* found the shorter path on every route, and the same path every time. RRT* varied. From the kitchen to the bedroom it used the short hallway in only of 30 runs. In the others it went around through the bathroom. The hallway is narrow and has a bend, so random points rarely land inside it before the trees meet another way.

A* is not always the shortest, though. From the bathroom to the bedroom the best RRT* run ( m) beat A* ( m). A* can only move between neighbouring squares, while RRT* can link two points at any angle.

RRT* was usually faster to plan here, but that is mostly because of the grid. A* spends most of its time building the grid, not searching it. In a small house that is cheap. In a big map, or one that changes often, the grid has to be rebuilt every time and the cost adds up.

Limits

This is one small flat house and a round robot that drives at a constant speed. The robots do not sense anything. When the hallway closes, they are told and plan again from where they are. PyRoboSim's RRT* stops as soon as its two trees meet. It does not keep improving the route if given more time, as RRT* can. The times were measured on one laptop and change from machine to machine. The path lengths do not.
